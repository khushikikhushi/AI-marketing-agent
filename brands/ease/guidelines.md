 — 03 Logo System 
 
 — The master identity must remain recognisable and unchanged. 

Primary master logo
The complete logo — geometric tree, E.A.S.E. wordmark and “EduArty Skills of Eminence” descriptor — is the preferred version for websites, proposals, brochures, certificates, presentations, institutional communication and other formal applications.
Secondary logo — Brand mark 
 
Tree symbol + E.A.S.E. wordmark. Use only where the descriptor would become too small to read. — The geometric tree may be used independently for favicons, profile icons, app icons or watermarks after the master identity is already visible or well established. 
Master artwork — Background preference 
 
Use approved original/vector artwork wherever possible. Do not redraw the tree or retype the E.A.S.E. wordmark. — White or a very light neutral is the default. On dark backgrounds, use an approved reversed/white-wordmark version once produced from the vector master. 
 — Important 
 
 — The current guideline is based on the supplied PNG master logo. Production colour values and alternate logo files should be finalised against the original editable/vector artwork before high-volume print production. 
 — 04 Logo Protection & Misuse 
 
 — Protect recognition, readability and consistency. 
Clear space
Maintain clear space equal to at least the height of the capital “E” in the E.A.S.E. wordmark on all four sides. No text, image, border or partner logo should enter this protected area.

Do not
Do not distort — Do not recolour 
 
Never stretch, squash, rotate or skew the logo. — Do not change individual geometric-tree colours or recolour the master wordmark. 
Do not decorate — Do not rewrite 
 
Do not add shadows, bevels, glows, outlines or gradients. — Do not recreate “E.A.S.E.” in another font. 
Do not crowd — Do not merge names 
 
Avoid placing the logo on busy photography or too close to other elements. — Do not insert “Mental Math” or another programme name inside the master logo artwork.

 — 07 Typography 
 
 — Simple, readable and consistent across Word, PowerPoint, print and digital. 
Primary typeface: Calibri
Calibri provides excellent cross-platform readability and keeps school, parent and internal documents consistent. Where Calibri is unavailable, use a metrically compatible sans-serif substitute while preserving hierarchy.
 Level Weight Typical size Use
 Display / Campaign Bold 28–44 pt Major marketing headlines
 Heading 1 Bold 20–28 pt Section titles
 Heading 2 Semibold 14–18 pt Subsections / key messages
 Body Regular 10–12 pt Documents, captions and detail
 Small / Meta Regular 8–9 pt Footers, labels, source notes
Typography rules
- Use sentence case for most headings; reserve ALL CAPS for short labels or programme identifiers.
- Keep line lengths comfortable and avoid dense blocks of text.
- Use bold for hierarchy, not for whole paragraphs.
- Avoid decorative, script or cartoon fonts in standard brand communication.
 — 08 Visual Language & Photography 
 
 — Modern, intelligent, human and visibly connected to learning. 
Visual direction
Bright — Premium 
 
Use light, clean backgrounds and strong negative space. — Avoid clutter, low-resolution imagery and tuition-centre visual clichés. 
Human — Modern 
 
Show real concentration, progress and achievement rather than exaggerated posing. — Digital learning, tablets and contemporary environments are appropriate when authentic. 
Geometric — Purposeful 
 
Use subtle angular crops and polygonal motifs inspired by the E.A.S.E. tree. — Every visual should support the learning message rather than simply decorate the page. 
Preferred photography
- Children actively solving or thinking
- Online or digital learning in authentic settings
- Close-ups that communicate focus and confidence
- Parent-child learning interactions
- Achievement moments, certificates and progress
- Genuine E.A.S.E. students and classrooms where consent permits
Avoid
Overly cartoonish visuals, excessive floating numbers, generic “genius child” imagery, cheap stock-photo expressions, rainbow colour overload, unrealistic face editing or effects that undermine credibility.
 — 09 Brand Voice & Messaging 
 
 — Credible for schools and parents; motivating for learners. 
Tone principles
Clear — Credible 
 
Use straightforward language and explain educational value. — Prefer measurable skills and specific outcomes over hype. 
Encouraging — Professional 
 
Frame learning as progress and capability building. — Suitable for parents, educators and school leadership. 
Accessible — Confident 
 
Avoid unnecessary jargon when communicating with families. — Make claims carefully and avoid exaggerated promises.

 — 11 Applications & Co-branding 
 
 — How the identity should behave across operational materials. 
Core applications
Certificates — Proposals & brochures 
 
Use the parent logo prominently, a clean white base, controlled programme naming and formal hierarchy. — Use E.A.S.E. as the master signature; introduce Mental Math as the programme title. 
Presentations — Worksheets & practice PDFs 
 
Keep slides minimal, with navy/teal section accents and the master logo in a consistent position. — Use simplified branding, strong readability and restrained decorative elements. 
Videos — School partnerships 
 
Use a short branded intro/outro; avoid placing the logo continuously over important learning content. — Maintain clear separation between E.A.S.E. and partner logos, equal optical respect and sufficient clear space. 
Co-branding rule
 — E.A.S.E. × Partner 
 
 — When E.A.S.E. appears with a school or institutional partner, neither logo should be visually merged with the other. Use a divider or neutral spacing, preserve each logo’s clear space, and avoid implying ownership or endorsement beyond the actual relationship.
